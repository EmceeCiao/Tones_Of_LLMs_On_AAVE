Great question! When it comes to mosquito bites, it definitely seems like some people get bitten a lot more than others—and there’s real science behind that.

Here are some of the main reasons why mosquitoes are more attracted to certain people:

  1. Body Chemistry: Mosquitoes are attracted to chemicals that our bodies naturally give off, such as lactic acid, uric acid, and ammonia, which are found in sweat. Some people naturally produce more of these chemicals, making them more appealing to mosquitoes.
  2. Body Temperature & Heat: People who run hotter or have a higher body temperature can attract more mosquitoes. Physical activity, for example, can make you more of a target because of the heat and sweat you emit.
  3. Carbon Dioxide (CO₂): Mosquitoes can detect the carbon dioxide we exhale from a distance. People who exhale more CO₂—like adults and larger individuals—might attract more bites.
  4. Blood Type: There’s some evidence that people with Type O blood get bitten more often than people with Type A or B blood.
  5. Skin Bacteria: The mix and amount of bacteria on your skin can create unique odors that attract mosquitoes. Some skin microbiomes are just more tantalizing to mosquitoes.
  6. Scents (Lotions, Perfume, etc.): Some scents from personal care products can either attract or repel mosquitoes, so things like fruity lotions or floral perfumes can make you more interesting to them.

So, it’s not exactly that some people “taste” better, but rather that the combination of body chemistry, smells, and heat makes some people more noticeable and inviting to mosquitoes than others.

If you’re one of those people who always seems to get bitten, it’s definitely not your imagination—there’s real biology behind it!
