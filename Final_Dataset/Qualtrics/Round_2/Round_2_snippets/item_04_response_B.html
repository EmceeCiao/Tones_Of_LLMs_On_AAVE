It’s really understandable to feel that way—sometimes humor, especially when it’s playful or a little rough around the edges, can be hard to interpret. The line between joking and disrespect can be subtle, and it’s totally normal to reflect on interactions after the fact.

Here are some ways to help tell the difference:

1. Pay attention to tone and context.

  • Jokes among friends can be teasing, but usually the tone is light and everyone’s in on the fun.
  • If the person’s tone felt sharp, or if the joke singled you out in a way that made you uncomfortable (especially if it happens repeatedly), that could be a red flag.

2. Think about your relationship with the person.

  • Good friends usually know your boundaries and don’t want to cross them.
  • If the person rarely jokes with others the way they joke with you, or if their comments tend to put you down, that might be less about fun and more about disrespect.

3. Reflect on how you felt in the moment and afterward.

  • If you laughed at the time but later felt uneasy, that’s a sign your gut is picking up that something wasn’t right for you.
  • Trusting your own feelings is important—even if others laughed too.

4. Notice patterns.

  • Does this person often make jokes at your expense, especially when others are around?
  • Occasional playful teasing is normal, but a consistent pattern of jokes that feel “off” could mean it’s more about putting you down.

5. Consider intention vs. impact.

  • Sometimes someone might mean it as a joke, but it still feels hurtful or disrespectful.
  • Even if their intention wasn’t bad, your feelings are valid.

What you can do:

  • If you’re close with the person, you might bring it up (“Hey, when you said __, I wasn’t sure if you were joking. It kind of stuck with me.”).
  • You could also let them know which types of jokes you’re cool with and which you aren’t.

Bottom line: If you’re questioning whether it was disrespectful, that feeling is worth listening to, even if you decide not to say anything right away. Setting boundaries is your right, and people who care about you will want to respect them. If you’re ever unsure, talking it through with a friend you trust can also help you get perspective.

You’re not alone in feeling this way—these situations can be tricky for anyone.
