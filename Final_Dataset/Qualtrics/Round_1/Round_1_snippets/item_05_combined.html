Prompt 5:

For most organisms, what is the dominant system of defense?

Response A:

For most organisms, especially animals, the innate immune system (also called innate immunity) is the dominant system of defense.

Key points:

  • Innate immunity is present in all multicellular organisms (animals, plants, fungi).
  • It is the first line of defense and is non-specific: it recognizes and responds to pathogens in a generic way.
  • Components include physical barriers (skin, cuticle, cell walls), chemical defenses (antimicrobial peptides, enzymes), and cellular defenses (phagocytes in animals).

Adaptive Immunity:

  • Adaptive (acquired) immunity is a more specialized and specific system found mainly in jawed vertebrates (like mammals, birds, reptiles, some fishes).
  • Most organisms do not possess adaptive immunity.

Plants & Invertebrates:

  • Plants and invertebrates (e.g., insects, worms) rely almost entirely on innate immunity for defense against pathogens.

Summary:
For most organisms, innate immunity is the dominant system of defense. Adaptive immunity is unique to jawed vertebrates and not found in the majority of life forms.

Response B:

For most living things, the immune system serves as the main defense system.

  • In animals (including humans), the immune system defends the body against pathogens like bacteria, viruses, and parasites. It consists of various cells, tissues, and organs (like white blood cells, lymph nodes, and the spleen) that detect and destroy invaders.
  • In plants, the defense system is made up of physical barriers (like cell walls and waxy coatings) and a specialized immune response involving recognition of pathogens and production of defensive chemicals.

In short:
The immune system is the primary defense system for most living organisms against diseases and harmful invaders.
