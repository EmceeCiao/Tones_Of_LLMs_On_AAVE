Prompt 11:

Someone I thought I was close with didn't invite me to something that everyone else went to, and I feel really hurt. Do I say something, or do I just let it go?

Response A:

I'm really sorry you're feeling this way—being left out hurts, especially when it's someone you thought you were close with.

Whether you should say something or let it go depends on a few factors:

1. How close are you really?
If this is someone you have a history of sharing feelings with, and if your friendship is usually open, it may be worth talking about it. Sometimes, bringing up your feelings can deepen trust and prevent future misunderstandings.

2. Is this a pattern, or a one-time thing?
If this is the first time, it might have been an oversight, or there might be a valid reason you weren't included that you don't know yet. If it’s happened more than once, you might need to address it.

3. How important is this event to you?
If the event was significant to you, or you find yourself thinking about it a lot, bottling up your feelings might cause resentment.

If you choose to say something:

  • Approach them calmly and when you’re not feeling overwhelmed.
  • Use “I” statements, e.g., “I felt a bit left out when I saw the group together and realized I wasn’t invited. I just wanted to check in about it.”
  • Their response will tell you a lot about how they value your friendship.

If you choose to let it go:

  • Make sure you’re not simply avoiding discomfort. Sometimes, letting go is healthy, especially if it was minor or unintentional.
  • Focus on nurturing other friendships and self-care.

No matter what, your feelings are valid. You deserve friends who are thoughtful and inclusive. Even if it was unintentional, being honest can only help clarify where you stand with this person.

If you want help wording a message or thinking through more specific details, feel free to share more!

Response B:

I’m really sorry you’re going through that. That kind of situation can definitely sting, especially when you care about the friendship. Here are a few things to consider:

1. Check how close you really are: Sometimes, people’s definition of “cool with” vs. “close friends” is different. It might be helpful to think about whether this was an intentional slight or maybe just an oversight.

2. Consider what you want out of speaking up:

  • Do you want to maintain the friendship, or does this change how you feel about them?
  • Are you looking for an explanation, apology, or just to let them know how it made you feel?

3. How much will it matter in a week or a month?
Give yourself a minute to process. Sometimes these things hurt in the moment but fade with time.

If you want to say something:

  • Approach it gently, without accusation. Something like, “Hey, I noticed I wasn’t invited to [event]—just want to check in, did I do something?” or “Not gonna lie, it felt kinda weird not being included.”
  • Gauge their response. If they’re dismissive or defensive, that says something about the friendship.

If you want to leave it:

  • It’s okay to take space and pull back your energy.
  • Focus on friends who make you feel included.

At the end of the day, you deserve to be around people who value and include you. If you’re still unsure, trust your gut—and know that it’s okay to protect your feelings.
