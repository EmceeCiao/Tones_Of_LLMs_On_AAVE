That’s a really relatable situation! When someone—especially your boss—says “Let’s revisit this later,” it can feel ambiguous. Sometimes it really does mean “not now but definitely later,” and sometimes it’s a polite way to move on and possibly not bring it up again.

How to interpret it:

  • It’s often intentionally vague to buy time or avoid a hard “no.”
  • Unless they've said “Absolutely, let’s schedule a time to follow up,” assume it could go either way.

What you can do:

  1. Follow up:
    Don't be afraid to bring it back up! You could say, “Hey, just checking in on [topic]. Did you want to revisit that now or schedule a time?”
  2. Take Initiative:
    If it’s important to you, set a reminder for yourself and follow up after a few days or the agreed initial time.
  3. Read the Context:
    • If your boss seemed rushed or distracted, they truly might just need time.
    • If they seemed dismissive or noncommittal, it might be their way of putting it off indefinitely.

How to “takeshi” it (take it):

  • Don’t take it personally; leaders often have a lot on their plate.
  • Assume neutrality—they didn’t say “no.” Use it as an opening to show initiative by circling back yourself.
  • If it keeps happening, you might frame your next follow-up as, “I’d like to make sure this doesn’t fall through the cracks—could we put it on the calendar?”

Summary:
It’s normal to feel unsure, but the best approach is courteous persistence. Be proactive, but flexible, and you’ll likely get clarity!
