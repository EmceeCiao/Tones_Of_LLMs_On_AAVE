Great question! In physics, when figuring out net force (the overall force acting on an object), the calculations often use vectors, which are usually represented as arrows in diagrams or as lines with direction and magnitude.

When it comes to equations and adding forces together, the "shapes" they use are often:

  1. Straight Line (Linear/Collinear Forces):
    • If all the forces act in a straight line (like a tug-of-war), you just add or subtract the forces (with direction as positive or negative).
    • Shape used: straight line.
  2. Parallelogram or Triangle (Non-Collinear Forces):
    • If the forces act at angles to each other, you often draw them as arrows from a common point and then use the parallelogram law or the triangle law to add vectors.
    • The most common shapes here are:
      • Triangle (triangle law of vector addition)
      • Parallelogram (parallelogram law of vector addition)

Summary Table:

SituationShape UsedDescription
Forces in one directionStraight LineForces added/subtracted linearly
Forces at anglesParallelogramCombine using parallelogram law
Forces at anglesTriangleCombine using triangle law

In Equations:
The net force is often written as:

\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_3 + \cdots

This equation implies you are adding vectors, which are usually represented using the shapes above.

In short:
They use lines, triangles, or parallelograms to represent and add forces in net force equations, depending on direction!
