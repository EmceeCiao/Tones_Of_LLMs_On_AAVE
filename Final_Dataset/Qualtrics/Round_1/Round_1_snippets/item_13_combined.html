Prompt 13:

I emailed my professor a question, and they replied with just “Please see the syllabus.” Was that rude, and how should I respond?

Response A:

It’s understandable to feel a bit dismissed when you get a short reply like “Please see the syllabus.” However, this response isn’t necessarily rude—it’s actually pretty common in academic settings.

Why professors do this:

  • Professors receive a lot of emails, many of which are about information clearly stated in the syllabus.
  • They want to encourage students to read and use the syllabus as a resource, and replying this way is a way to reinforce that habit.

How to respond:

  • Take a moment to reread the syllabus carefully, focusing on the section relevant to your question.
  • If you find the answer, there’s no need to reply further.
  • If, after checking, your question still isn’t answered or you’re confused by what’s written, you can reply briefly and politely, for example:

Thank you for your reply. I reread the syllabus, but I’m still unclear about [specific part]. Could you please clarify [your specific question]? Thank you for your help!

Key tips:

  • Don’t take it personally; it’s not a reflection of their opinion of you.
  • Always try to find answers in the syllabus or course materials first.

Response B:

It’s understandable to feel a little brushed off by a “Please see the syllabus” reply—it can feel abrupt! However, many professors receive a huge volume of emails, and they often get questions that are already answered in the syllabus. Their response is probably an effort to encourage students to use the syllabus as a resource and to manage their time effectively.

Is it rude?
Not necessarily. It’s probably just brief and to the point, rather than intentionally rude.

How should you respond?
You don’t have to reply if your question is answered after checking the syllabus. But if you want to keep things positive (which is always good!), you could send a brief, polite response like:

Example response:

Thank you, Professor [Name]. I apologize for missing that information—I’ll be sure to check the syllabus more carefully in the future.

This shows you respect their time and are willing to learn from the interaction.

Going forward…

  • Always double-check the syllabus before asking questions.
  • If you can’t find the answer, you can say in your future emails, “I checked the syllabus, but couldn’t find the answer to [specific question],” which shows you did your homework.

Summary:
Their response wasn’t personal! Stay positive and use it as a learning moment. If you want to reply, keep it polite and brief.
