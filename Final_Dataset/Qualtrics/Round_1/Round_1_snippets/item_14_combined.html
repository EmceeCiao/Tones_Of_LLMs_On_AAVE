Prompt 14:

I keep being the one who reaches out first in my friendships, and it's starting to make me feel unwanted. How do I bring that up without sounding needy?

Response A:

That’s a very real and common feeling, and it’s great that you want to address it thoughtfully. Bringing it up can absolutely be done in a way that’s open and honest without coming across as needy. Here’s how you might approach it:

1. Choose the right time and person:
Pick a close, trusted friend, and a moment where you can talk privately, not over text if possible—sometimes sensitive conversations go better voice-to-voice.

2. Use “I” statements:
Frame your feelings from your own perspective. For example:
“Hey, I’ve noticed I’ve been the one reaching out first most of the time, and sometimes it makes me wonder if you still want to hang out as much. I care about our friendship and just wanted to check in—am I overthinking it?”

3. Avoid accusatory language:
Don’t say, “You never text me first” or “You clearly don’t care.” Instead, stick to what you’ve observed:
“I realized I usually start our conversations, and I guess I feel a bit self-conscious about it.”

4. Ask for their perspective:
Give your friend a chance to respond. They might not realize you feel this way, or there could be a reason for their behavior:
“Is there a reason you don’t text as much, or is it just how you are in friendships?”

5. Suggest what you’d like to see:
Rather than demanding, gently suggest solutions:
“It would mean a lot if you’d reach out sometimes too. It helps me feel connected.”

6. Keep the tone positive:
Remind them why you value your bond:
“I really love talking to you and spending time together.”

Sample script:

“Hey! I wanted to share something on my mind—I’ve noticed I usually reach out first, and sometimes it makes me worry I might be bothering you or that you’re less interested in hanging out. I love our friendship and just wanted to check in and see how you feel about it. Is that something you’ve noticed too?”

Friendships do ebb and flow. If your friend responds well, great! If not, it might be a sign to invest energy in people who reciprocate—but opening the door first is always worth it.

Response B:

That’s really tough, and your feelings are totally valid. Wanting reciprocity in your relationships doesn’t make you clingy—it means you care and want to feel valued!

Here’s a gentle, honest way to bring it up:

1. Focus on how you feel, not what they do “wrong.”
Example:
“Hey, I’ve noticed I’m usually the one reaching out first, and sometimes it makes me worry that I’m more invested than others. I just wanted to share how I’m feeling because I value our friendship/relationship.”

2. Don’t make demands—invite conversation.
Ask something like:
“Have you noticed that too? Or is that just my perception?”

3. Be open to their side.
Sometimes people get distracted or assume you don’t mind starting conversations. Giving them a chance to explain can help.

A sample message:
“Hey, can I be honest with you about something? Lately I’ve felt like I’m usually the one reaching out, and it’s started to make me feel a bit unwanted. I really appreciate our connection, so I just wanted to mention it. Is that something you’ve noticed too?”

This frames it as you sharing your feelings, not blaming them or begging for attention.

Bonus:
If they care about you, they’ll want to know how you feel and (hopefully!) meet you halfway. And if not… that gives you some useful info, too.

You deserve friendships that go both ways. 💛
