Response A:

I'm really sorry you're feeling this way—being left out hurts, especially when it's someone you thought you were close with.

Whether you should say something or let it go depends on a few factors:

1. How close are you really?
If this is someone you have a history of sharing feelings with, and if your friendship is usually open, it may be worth talking about it. Sometimes, bringing up your feelings can deepen trust and prevent future misunderstandings.

2. Is this a pattern, or a one-time thing?
If this is the first time, it might have been an oversight, or there might be a valid reason you weren't included that you don't know yet. If it’s happened more than once, you might need to address it.

3. How important is this event to you?
If the event was significant to you, or you find yourself thinking about it a lot, bottling up your feelings might cause resentment.

If you choose to say something:

  • Approach them calmly and when you’re not feeling overwhelmed.
  • Use “I” statements, e.g., “I felt a bit left out when I saw the group together and realized I wasn’t invited. I just wanted to check in about it.”
  • Their response will tell you a lot about how they value your friendship.

If you choose to let it go:

  • Make sure you’re not simply avoiding discomfort. Sometimes, letting go is healthy, especially if it was minor or unintentional.
  • Focus on nurturing other friendships and self-care.

No matter what, your feelings are valid. You deserve friends who are thoughtful and inclusive. Even if it was unintentional, being honest can only help clarify where you stand with this person.
