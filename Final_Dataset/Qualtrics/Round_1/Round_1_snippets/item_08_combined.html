Prompt 8:

When I delete a picture or program, what actually happens to it?

Response A:

Great question! When you delete a picture or program on your computer, phone, or other digital device, a few things happen under the hood. Here’s a simple breakdown:

1. Deleting from Your Device

Computer (e.g., Windows Trash Bin/Recycling Bin, macOS Trash):

  • When you hit “delete,” the file isn’t wiped from the drive immediately.
  • Instead, the space where it’s stored is simply marked as “available” for new data.
  • The file may remain in a recycling bin/trash folder until you empty it.

Smartphones/Tablets:

  • Deleted photos often go to a “Recently Deleted” or “Trash” album for 30-60 days before being permanently deleted.

2. What REALLY Happens

  • The “pointer” or directory entry is removed, so the file is no longer visible to everyday users or most programs.
  • The actual bits of the file sit on your storage media (hard drive, SSD, flash memory) until that space gets overwritten by new data.

3. Permanently Deleting

  • When you empty the trash/recycle bin, or delete a file permanently:
    • The system still usually just marks the space as reusable.
    • The data can still be recovered with special recovery tools until the space actually gets overwritten.

4. Why Doesn’t It Vanish Instantly?

  • Overwriting data immediately would take extra time and wear out your drive more quickly.
  • This approach makes deleting files look instant and keeps your device running smoothly.

5. Special Cases

  • Solid State Drives (SSDs) and newer operating systems use a command called TRIM to more efficiently clear deleted data, but even then, recovery might be possible for a while.
  • Cloud services (Google Photos, OneDrive, etc.) usually have a similar "trash" period; files may also persist in backups for a time.
  • Uninstalling programs typically just removes system references and associated files, but might also leave behind leftovers (e.g., settings, cache).

6. If You Want to Make Sure It’s Gone

  • Use secure "shredding" tools or built-in utilities ("Secure Erase") to overwrite the file’s data, making recovery extremely difficult.

In a Nutshell

Deleting just tells your device “you can use this space for something else.” The data sticks around until it’s overwritten.

If you want to make recovery nearly impossible, you have to securely erase/wipe the data.

Response B:

Great question! Here’s what happens when you delete a picture or an app on your device:

When You Delete a Picture

  1. Removed from Viewing: The photo disappears from your gallery or photo app.
  2. Moved to “Trash” or “Recently Deleted” Folder (most phones/computers):
    • On iPhones and many Androids, deleted photos are moved to a “Recently Deleted” folder for about 30 days before permanent deletion.
  3. Permanent Deletion:
    • After the holding period (or if you "empty trash" manually), the file’s entry is removed from the file system.
    • Data Remains on Storage - for a while:
      • The actual image data isn’t immediately erased. The space it occupied is marked as “available” for new data.
      • Data Recovery Tools sometimes can recover recently deleted files—unless the storage space is overwritten.

When You Delete an App

  1. Icon and App Data Removed:
    • The app’s icon disappears from your home screen/app drawer.
    • App data stored locally is usually deleted.
  2. Account/Cloud Data Might Stay:
    • If you’ve signed up for the app or stored data online (cloud backups, login info), your account and some settings may remain online until you delete them from within the app/service website.
  3. Storage Freed Up:
    • The files used by the app are erased (like with pictures--actually marked as “free space”).
    • Your device can use that space for other things.
  4. Permissions Revoked:
    • The app can no longer access your device’s resources (camera, location, etc.).

Key Takeaways

  • On the surface: The item disappears from where you usually see it.
  • Under the hood: The storage space is marked as available, but the actual data lingers until it’s overwritten.
  • Permanently deleting (overwriting) is sometimes possible with “secure erase” or special tools.

Tips

  • To ensure sensitive photos or data are unrecoverable, look for “secure delete” options or factory reset the device if you’re giving it away.
  • Deleting an app won’t affect your account data on their servers unless you specifically delete your account through the app or website.
