If your iPhone is shutting down before the battery reaches 0%—sometimes even at 20%—that’s a sign the battery is likely degraded or there's a calibration issue. This is pretty common in older devices or devices with worn-out batteries.

Why is this happening?

1. Battery Aging

  • Lithium-ion batteries wear out over time, usually after a couple of years or a certain number of charge cycles.
  • As they age, their ability to hold a charge and provide consistent voltage drops, causing unexpected shutdowns.

2. Battery Calibration Issue

  • Sometimes, the battery percentage displayed becomes inaccurate and doesn’t reflect the actual charge remaining, leading the phone to shut off when it thinks it's still at 20%.

3. Extreme Temperatures

  • Using your phone in very hot or cold environments can make these issues more noticeable.

4. Software Issues

  • Occasionally, iOS glitches or bugs might cause inaccurate charge readings.

How to FIX it

1. Check Battery Health

  • Go to Settings > Battery > Battery Health (or Battery Health & Charging, in iOS 16+).
  • If Maximum Capacity is below 80% or it says “Service”, your battery is degraded.

2. Calibrate the Battery

Try to recalibrate the battery:

  1. Charge your iPhone to 100% (leave it plugged in for an extra hour after it reaches 100%).
  2. Use your phone until it dies completely (shuts down on its own).
  3. Charge it uninterrupted to 100% again.

This sometimes helps the software re-learn what 0% and 100% truly are.

3. Avoid Extreme Temperatures

Keep your phone away from very hot or cold places while using or charging.

4. Update iOS

Sometimes Apple releases updates that address battery issues. Check for updates in Settings > General > Software Update.

5. Replace the Battery

If calibration doesn't help AND battery health shows as degraded (below 80% or “Service”), you should consider a battery replacement. You can do this:

  • At an Apple Store
  • At an authorized service provider
  • Or third-party repair shops (less recommended for newer devices).

Bottom Line

Most likely your battery is worn out or needs recalibrating. If the problem doesn't go away after calibrating and updating, replacing the battery will usually fix it!
